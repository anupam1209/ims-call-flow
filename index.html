01

IMS Registration — precondition

REGISTER · UAR/MAR/SAR · 3rd-party REGISTER

Before any call can be placed the UE has to register with the IMS core. Registration is what gives the S-CSCF the subscriber's iFC profile, and that profile is what triggers the TAS on every later INVITE.

Round 1UE → P-CSCF → I-CSCF: REGISTER. The I-CSCF uses UAR/UAA (Diameter Cx) to find out which S-CSCF will serve the subscriber. The S-CSCF fetches the AKA authentication vectors with MAR/MAA and answers with a 401 Unauthorized challenge.

Round 2The UE sends REGISTER again, this time with computed credentials. The S-CSCF downloads the user profile and iFC list with SAR/SAA, matches an iFC and sends a 3rd-party REGISTER to the TAS, and 200 OK travels back to the UE. The P-CSCF stores the Service-Route header — which is why every later INVITE goes straight to the S-CSCF.

02

VoLTE Call — Originating Side

§8.2 · Fig. 33 · UE1 dials UE2

The SIP INVITE arrives at the P-CSCF, which forwards it to the selected S-CSCF. The S-CSCF identifies the VoLTE call from the iFC received during registration and forwards the INVITE to the TAS. The TAS checks the UE status and the subscriber's originating features and routes the INVITE back to the S-CSCF; alongside that it queries the HSS over Sh with UDR/UDA for the MME/SGSN name and Cell-Id.

ENUM / NPSThe S-CSCF determines the called party's home network with a NAPTR query to the IMS-ENUM+NPS server. The response returns the subscriber's domain / lrn and the call is routed on it. If that lrn is not served by the home network, the call is sent to the GMSC via the IBCF.

183 → bearerWhen 183 Session Progress comes back from the terminating user, the P-CSCF analyses the SDP and sends an AAR to the PCRF with the related service information — IP address, port numbers, media type. The PCRF authorises the request (AAA) and triggers creation of the voice bearer. The same exchange happens again on the 200 OK for the UPDATE.

PRACK · UPDATEUE1 generates a PRACK to acknowledge the reliable 183, and after its 200 OK it sends an UPDATE that locks the final SDP. After 180 Ringing the subscriber answers, 200 OK comes back, and RTP media starts flowing on the dedicated bearer.

BYEUE1 hangs up. The BYE reaches the TAS, which queries the HSS for location information (UDR/UDA) before the BYE continues to the terminating side. The P-CSCF sends STR to the PCRF to release the voice bearer and receives STA; 200 OK then closes every dialog.

03

VoLTE Call — Terminating Side

§8.4 · Fig. 35 · INVITE delivered to UE2

The INVITE from the originating side arrives at the terminating I-CSCF. The I-CSCF finds UE2's S-CSCF through an HSS query — LIR/LIA over Diameter Cx — and routes the INVITE to it. The S-CSCF applies the terminating iFC and passes the INVITE to the TAS.

TAS ↔ HSS (Sh)The TAS makes three queries: TADS — TADS = 1 means the user is in the 4G / PS domain; UserState — whether the user is reachable; and Location-Info — MME/SGSN name and Cell-Id. Once these clear, the TAS applies the terminating features (CFU, call waiting, MT barring, PRBT) and the INVITE travels back S-CSCF → P-CSCF → UE2.

183 → bearerUE2 answers with 183 Session Progress. The P-CSCF authorises the dedicated voice bearer with AAR/AAA, and the 183 retraces the request path back through S-CSCF → TAS → S-CSCF → I-CSCF to the originating side.

AnswerPRACK and UPDATE arrive from the originating side and UE2 answers 200 OK to each; the UPDATE answer triggers a second AAR/AAA round. UE2 rings (180 Ringing) and when the subscriber answers, the 200 OK establishes the RTP media session between the two users.

BYEThe BYE arrives from the originating side. The TAS queries the HSS for location information and the BYE is delivered to UE2. The P-CSCF releases the bearer with STR/STA and 200 OK closes every leg.
